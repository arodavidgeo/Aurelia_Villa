## 2. Visual theme and atmosphere

The design is an elegant, boutique-villa hospitality website. It should feel quiet, warm, refined, architectural, nature-connected, and expensive without looking flashy. The visual mood comes from:
- Warm ivory backgrounds and paper-like surfaces.
- Deep olive-green text and footer surfaces.
- Muted antique-gold accents and hairline rules.
- Refined editorial serif headlines.
- Small, letter-spaced uppercase labels.
- Fine botanical line art at page edges and in whitespace.
- Rich, natural-looking villa photography with golden sunlight.
- Generous whitespace and thin, discreet borders.
- Small, restrained controls and low-radius corners.

Density: airy/editorial, with moderate detail in the booking and room-listing areas.
Layout variance: balanced and editorial, using image/text splits and asymmetric visual weight.
Motion: restrained. The reference is a static design board; do not let motion change its appearance.

## 4. Typography

The reference combines high-contrast editorial serif display typography with a clean, understated sans-serif for navigation, labels, controls, and utility text.

- **Display and section headlines:** use a refined modern editorial serif, preferably `Cormorant Garamond` or the closest visually matching available serif. Use regular weight, graceful contrast, and tight but legible line spacing.
- **Brand wordmark:** use a fine, elegant serif with generous tracking. Keep the Villa Aurelia monogram and wordmark proportions faithful to the supplied logo/reference.
- **Body copy:** use a restrained, readable sans-serif or light editorial text style matching the reference. Keep body copy small, calm, and low contrast without becoming illegible.
- **Navigation and metadata:** small uppercase labels with generous letter spacing.
- **Buttons:** small uppercase text, modest tracking, and no heavy bold weight.
- **Prices:** serif numerals, aligned to the right in room rows.
- **Avoid:** oversized heavy sans-serif headlines, playful fonts, generic startup typography, overly bold body copy, and inconsistent font pairings.

Typography should look editorial and calm, never like a dashboard or a generic booking marketplace.

## 16. Buttons, controls, and component styling

- **Primary action:** antique-gold fill, dark/ivory text according to contrast, square or minimally rounded corners, small uppercase label.
- **Secondary action:** transparent/ivory fill, thin antique-gold outline, understated text.
- **Navigation:** plain text links, no pill backgrounds.
- **Filters:** small, low-profile controls; only active filters use deep olive fill.
- **Booking strip:** thin gold outline, light fill, distinct field separators, gold line icons.
- **Room rows:** horizontal layout with a fine outline or subtle ivory surface, no heavy shadows.
- **Forms:** label above each input; thin borders; compact controls; clear focus and inline validation states.
- **Icons:** simple fine-line icons in muted gold; no emoji icons.
- **Borders:** thin and warm; use them to separate content rather than adding heavy shadows.
- **Shadows:** nearly imperceptible, only where visible in the reference.
- **Corner radius:** restrained. Do not use oversized rounded cards or pill-shaped buttons.

## 17. Motion and interaction

The reference is a quiet, static editorial layout. Interaction should be functional but visually restrained:
- Navigation links have a subtle colour transition.
- Buttons provide a small tactile pressed state.
- Filter controls visibly show their active selection.
- Date and guest controls open clear, accessible selectors.
- Gallery images can open a lightbox if the implementation requires it, without changing the gallery's resting design.
- Forms provide clear success and inline error states.
- Motion must never change the reference layout, create overlapping content, or delay access to booking information.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- No custom cursors, bouncing arrows, excessive parallax, large scroll animations, or perpetual decorative animation.
